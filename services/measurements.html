Measurements

Independent laboratory data on what is actually in a supplement or raw material, so you can compare it with the label and the supplier’s claims.

Powder X-ray diffraction (XRD)

XRD gives a fingerprint of the crystalline phases in a powder. It shows which compound and which solid form you really have.

  • Identity: does the pattern match the expected compound?
  • Polymorph and salt or chelate form, for example bisglycinate versus oxide
  • Detection of crystalline adulterants or fillers
  • Comparison of batches or suppliers

HPLC

High-performance liquid chromatography separates and quantifies the components of a sample, so you know how much of the active compound is present.

  • Assay: content of the active compound versus the label claim
  • Purity and related impurities
  • Batch-to-batch consistency
  • Method and conditions documented for repeatability

Reports

Every measurement ends in a written report you can file, share with a supplier or attach to a regulatory dossier.

  • Sample description and identification
  • Method, instrument and conditions used
  • Results with a clear comparison to the label or specification
  • Raw data and chromatograms or diffractograms attached
  • A plain-language conclusion
